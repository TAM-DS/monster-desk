# 9am hiring-manager loop (8–10 minutes)

Open with the sentence they already heard:

> I was a trader at Lehman. I did not build agents that trade. I built the desk that will not let them.

Then:

**What I built before this call**  
Monster Heavy — paper book, one Proposal Agent, human approval, policy rechecked at execute, Postgres lease so a dead worker cannot double-fill, compensation is a new ticket not a rollback. No HTTP mutations. No UI. That was deliberate.

**What this console is**  
Four seats over that contract. Research proposes. Risk binds a digest. Execution may only submit that digest. Surveillance records a halt. If Execution tries to change the name at the wire, the desk refuses.

**What I will not say**  
That agents ran overnight. That this is a live OMS. That six MCP servers are the architecture. That simulated fill equals street fill.

**If they ask “why not autonomous?”**  
Because on a real floor the person who has the view does not have the wire, and the person who has the wire does not get to change the ticket. I am not going to unlearn that for a demo.

**If they ask “where is the model?”**  
In Heavy it proposes terms. It does not approve, lease, or fill. Same split as Aegis: recommendation is not authorization.

**Close**  
The interesting platform problem at Morgan Stanley is not making an agent faster. It is making sure capability cannot quietly become authority when the process dies or the model is fluent.
